About this program

Join one of India's most impactful wildlife conservation programs in the heart of Ranthambore National Park. Over four weeks you'll work alongside field biologists and park rangers to monitor tiger populations, restore degraded habitats, and engage local communities in conservation. This is not tourism with a green veneer — it is real, unglamorous, meaningful fieldwork.

4-Week Program Schedule

Each week has a defined focus. Days run from 05:30 field start to evening debrief. Weekends include rest time and optional excursions.

A Typical Volunteering Day

Morning: Team brief, beach restoration activity

Noon: Lunch

Afternoon: Public awareness on beach and natural habitat conservation

Evening: Cooking, Dinner

Key Responsibilities

Bycatch Reduction: Organizations like NOAA Fisheries and WWF work with commercial fisheries to mandate Turtle Excluder Devices (TEDs) in nets and promote the use of barbless circle hooks.

Nesting Beach Protection: Patrolling beaches ensures eggs are safe from poachers and natural predators.

Community Engagement: Empowering local fishing communities to become conservation stewards is vital.

Habitat & Debris Management: Preserving seagrass beds and coral reefs is essential, as these serve as critical foraging grounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Application Process
How competitive is the application? ▾

We receive 50–80 applications per batch and select 12. We prioritise commitment and motivation over qualifications. A well-written motivation section matters more than a CV.

How long does the review process take? ▾

We review applications on a rolling basis and aim to respond within 5 working days. Shortlisted applicants will have a short 20-minute video call with our program coordinator.

Can I apply as a group or with a friend? ▾

Yes. Groups of up to 4 can apply together using our group application. Each person must complete their own profile and motivation section — but your applications are reviewed together and we try to accept full groups when possible.

Life at Camp
What are the living conditions like? ▾

Basic but comfortable. Shared 6-person dorms, common bathroom facilities with hot water, a communal mess area, and reliable electricity. WiFi is available in the evening only — daytime field hours are screen-free. This is not a hotel, but it is safe and well-maintained.

What if I need to leave early? ▾

We understand emergencies happen. In case of a genuine emergency you may leave early without penalty. Leaving for non-emergency reasons forfeits any remaining stipend. We take commitment seriously — please only apply if you can complete the full program.

Is there free time? Can I explore Ranthambore? ▾

Sundays are rest days and you can explore Sawai Madhopur town or the park gate area on your own. WCI organises one optional group excursion per batch. Personal safari bookings are discouraged during program weeks — you'll see more wildlife doing fieldwork than on any safari.

Stipend & Logistics
When and how is the stipend paid? ▾

Stipend of ₹5,000 is paid via NEFT on the last working day of the month. You must have an Indian bank account. International volunteers can arrange a transfer to a trusted local account or use a borderless banking service — our coordinator will assist.

How do I get to Ranthambore? ▾

The nearest station is Sawai Madhopur Railway Junction, well connected to Delhi (3.5 hrs), Jaipur (2 hrs), and Mumbai. WCI will arrange a pickup from the station on your arrival day. We send full travel guidance 2 weeks before your start date.
