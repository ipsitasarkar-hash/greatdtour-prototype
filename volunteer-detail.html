About this program

Join one of India's most impactful wildlife conservation programs in the heart of Ranthambore National Park. Over four weeks you'll work alongside field biologists and park rangers to monitor tiger populations, restore degraded habitats, and engage local communities in conservation. This is not tourism with a green veneer — it is real, unglamorous, meaningful fieldwork.

4-Week Program Schedule

Each week has a defined focus. Days run from 05:30 field start to evening debrief. Weekends include rest time and optional excursions.

Weeks 1–4: Full Program Breakdown
WK1
Orientation & Field Fundamentals
Getting your bearings in the park and mastering the tools.
  • Park induction with head biologist Dr. Rajan Mehta
  • Camera trap placement and maintenance training
  • Introduction to wildlife tracking and scat identification
  • Field safety protocols and zone geography
  • First data collection exercise (transect walk)
WK2
Wildlife Census & Monitoring
Deep fieldwork on tiger population tracking and prey base analysis.
  • Morning camera trap rounds (4:30 am starts)
  • Individual tiger ID from stripe pattern analysis
  • Prey density count — sambar, chital, nilgai
  • Waterhole activity logging and occupancy maps
  • Evening data entry and pattern review sessions
WK3
Habitat Restoration
Hands-on ecological work — replanting, waterhole maintenance, invasive species removal.
  • Native sapling planting in degraded zones (approx. 500 trees)
  • Invasive Lantana removal from core tiger corridors
  • Waterhole deepening and desilting with hand tools
  • Soil moisture and vegetation cover mapping
  • Before/after photographic documentation
WK4
Community Outreach & Reporting
Village engagement, education sessions, and presenting your field findings.
  • Wildlife education workshops in 3 buffer-zone villages
  • Human–wildlife conflict mediation training
  • Eco-tourism livelihood sessions for local families
  • Individual field report writing (submitted to WCI)
  • Closing ceremony and certificate presentation

Financial Commitment

We believe cost should not be a barrier to meaningful contribution. Here is exactly what this program costs and provides.

Fully Covered by WCI
What You Get — At No Cost
Wildlife Conservation India covers all program operational costs.
  • Shared accommodation in the Ranthambore base camp (6-person dorms)
  • Three meals per day — simple, nutritious field camp food
  • All field equipment: binoculars, GPS, camera traps, data notebooks
  • National Park entry permits and vehicle costs for all field days
  • Professional training and mentorship from senior biologists
  • Emergency medical support and first aid on-site
₹0 program fee
Monthly Stipend
You Receive ₹5,000 / month
A modest stipend to cover personal expenses and transport within Sawai Madhopur town.
  • Paid at the end of each calendar month
  • Directly to your bank account (NEFT)
  • Applicable to volunteers completing at least 3 of 4 weeks
Your Responsibility
What You Arrange
These are not large costs, but plan ahead.
  • Travel to Sawai Madhopur Railway Station (WCI will arrange pickup from there)
  • Personal travel insurance covering field activities (we can recommend providers)
  • Personal clothing appropriate for outdoor work (list provided on acceptance)
Financial Aid Available. For volunteers who cannot afford travel to Ranthambore, WCI offers limited travel grants. Mention this in your application — it does not affect your selection chances.

Frequently Asked Questions

Application Process
How competitive is the application? ▾

We receive 50–80 applications per batch and select 12. We prioritise commitment and motivation over qualifications. A well-written motivation section matters more than a CV.

How long does the review process take? ▾

We review applications on a rolling basis and aim to respond within 5 working days. Shortlisted applicants will have a short 20-minute video call with our program coordinator.

Can I apply as a group or with a friend? ▾

Yes. Groups of up to 4 can apply together using our group application. Each person must complete their own profile and motivation section — but your applications are reviewed together and we try to accept full groups when possible.

Life at Camp
What are the living conditions like? ▾

Basic but comfortable. Shared 6-person dorms, common bathroom facilities with hot water, a communal mess area, and reliable electricity. WiFi is available in the evening only — daytime field hours are screen-free. This is not a hotel, but it is safe and well-maintained.

What if I need to leave early? ▾

We understand emergencies happen. In case of a genuine emergency you may leave early without penalty. Leaving for non-emergency reasons forfeits any remaining stipend. We take commitment seriously — please only apply if you can complete the full program.

Is there free time? Can I explore Ranthambore? ▾

Sundays are rest days and you can explore Sawai Madhopur town or the park gate area on your own. WCI organises one optional group excursion per batch. Personal safari bookings are discouraged during program weeks — you'll see more wildlife doing fieldwork than on any safari.

Stipend & Logistics
When and how is the stipend paid? ▾

Stipend of ₹5,000 is paid via NEFT on the last working day of the month. You must have an Indian bank account. International volunteers can arrange a transfer to a trusted local account or use a borderless banking service — our coordinator will assist.

How do I get to Ranthambore? ▾

The nearest station is Sawai Madhopur Railway Junction, well connected to Delhi (3.5 hrs), Jaipur (2 hrs), and Mumbai. WCI will arrange a pickup from the station on your arrival day. We send full travel guidance 2 weeks before your start date.
